Project 1 - Objective 2 - Cylinder detection


0. Running

  ros2 launch cylinder_detector cylinder_detector.launch.py

(with the same nodes as in floor_plane_mapping/evaluation.txt). Outputs:

  ~/cylinders        visualization_msgs/MarkerArray, frame world, one CYLINDER
                     and one text label per cylinder of the map
  ~/vertical_points  points kept as vertical structure, useful for tuning

When the node stops it prints the final list of cylinders.


1. Method

The cylinders of the scene stand on the floor, so their axis is vertical and the
problem reduces to finding circles in the projection of the cloud on the ground.
The floor itself is not flat (two plateaus, a ramp and cliffs), so it is not
removed with a single plane.

a) The cloud is transformed into the world frame (TF at the cloud stamp).

b) Vertical structure. A local grid of 0.05 m cells is built around the sensor
   (3.5 m range) and each cell keeps z_min and z_max of its points. A vertical
   surface stacks points of many heights in the same cell, the ground does not:
   over 0.05 m the ramp (13 deg) rises 1 cm and the steepest cliff (60 deg)
   9 cm, while the side of a pillar spans tens of centimetres. A cell is
   vertical when z_max - z_min > 0.2 m. In those cells the floor points that
   share the cell with the object (z < z_min + 3 cm) are dropped.

c) Clusters are the connected components (cv::connectedComponents, 8-connected)
   of the image of vertical cells.

d) Circle fit on each cluster with at least 30 points:
   - RANSAC, 200 iterations: circle through 3 random points (circumcircle), kept
     if its radius is in [0.07, 0.3] m; score = points within 1 cm of the circle.
   - Least squares refit of x^2 + y^2 + D x + E y + F = 0 on the inliers, solved
     with the normal equations and ldlt as in the regression homework, in
     coordinates centred on the inliers so that the system is well conditioned:
       center = (-D/2, -E/2),  r = sqrt(D^2/4 + E^2/4 - F)

e) Acceptance tests on the refitted circle:
   - at least 30 points within 1 cm of the circle;
   - among the points closer than r + 5 cm to the center, at least 90 % are
     within 1 cm of the circle (a box or a piece of wall leaves many points off
     the circle in that region; a cylinder touching a wall still passes since
     the test only looks near the circle);
   - the inliers cover an arc of at least 90 deg (rejects short flat faces);
   - the center is farther from the sensor than the inliers, i.e. the surface
     is convex towards the sensor;
   - the inliers span at least 0.4 m vertically.

f) Map. Each detection is associated with the closest cylinder of the map whose
   center is within max(r_map, r_det) + 0.15 m, or creates a new one. Position
   and radius are averaged with weights w = 1/d^2, d being the distance from the
   sensor: the lateral error is driven by the pixel footprint, which grows like
   d, so the variance grows like d^2. The vertical extent is the union of the
   observed extents. A cylinder is published once it has been detected in 3
   frames, so that a single wrong fit never reaches the map. All the cylinders
   found since the start are kept and republished at every frame with stable
   ids.


2. Tuning

A bag of depth images and TF was recorded during a full explore_scan.py mission
(151 s) and replayed through the detector with different settings:

  min_inlier_ratio  min_height   pillars   false detections
  0.7               0.3          6 / 6     9  (3 plant pots, 2 boxes, 4 sofa arms)
  0.9               0.3          6 / 6     1  (sofa armrest, 0.31 m high)
  0.9               0.4          6 / 6     0

Why the boxes passed at 0.7: on a simulated 0.2 m square seen from 0.8 to 2.5 m
under different angles, the best circle keeps 60 to 70 % of the nearby points,
while a true cylinder keeps 100 % since the simulated depth has no noise. 0.9
separates the two clearly. On real data the tolerance would have to follow the
sensor noise.

The plants stand in a cylindrical pot (radius 0.075 m, 0.165 m high) under the
leaves. They are not reported on purpose: the leaf points around the pot keep
the local ratio below 0.9 (they disappeared when the ratio went from 0.7 to
0.9). Lowering the ratio would bring them back, together with the boxes.


3. Results

Final live run, ground truth read from the scene file:

  pillar   true x, y          true r   estimated x, y      est. r   error
  P        (-2.575,  3.175)   0.175    (-2.575,  3.173)    0.172    2 mm
  P0       (-3.025, -3.000)   0.125    (-3.024, -3.000)    0.123    1 mm
  P1       ( 4.075,  0.325)   0.100    ( 4.078,  0.327)    0.098    4 mm
  P2       ( 0.400,  4.775)   0.125    ( 0.398,  4.775)    0.121    2 mm
  P3       (-2.675,  0.150)   0.110    (-2.677,  0.151)    0.107    2 mm
  P4       ( 0.775,  1.950)   0.125    ( 0.773,  1.950)    0.119    2 mm

All 6 pillars are found, with no false detection. The radius is underestimated
by 2 to 6 mm, probably because the pillar meshes are faceted and the true radius
above is taken from their bounding box, i.e. at the vertices. P2 touches the
wall and is still found, thanks to the local inlier ratio.

1. The scene

The floor of rosControlKinect3d.ttt is a 64x64 heightfield. Reading it from the
scene file gives two plateaus at z = -0.5 (south) and z = +0.5 (north), joined
in the middle by a ramp of about 13 deg (through node 0), with cliffs of 30 to
60 deg on both sides of the ramp. Obstacles: 6 cylindrical pillars, 3 square
boxes 0.2 x 0.2 x 1.0, 2 sofas, 3 plants, and the walls at +-5 m.

So "traversable" has to accept the ramp and reject the cliffs. max_slope = 20
deg sits between the two with margin on both sides.


2. Method (objective 1)

The cloud is transformed into the world frame with TF at the stamp of the cloud
(world -> odom -> bubbleRob -> Vision_sensor). The grid is 110 x 110 cells of
0.1 m, which covers the 10 x 10 m arena plus 0.5 m of margin.

Peas in buckets: every point between 0.3 and 3.0 m from the sensor goes into the
bucket of its cell. A bucket does not keep the list of points but only

  n,  S = sum(p),  SS = sum(p p^T)

which is all that is needed for the mean m = S/n and the covariance
C = SS/n - m m^T. The points are taken relative to the center of the cell so
that SS does not lose precision.

For each cell with at least 6 points, C is diagonalised (PCA):

  normal     = eigenvector of the smallest eigenvalue l0
  slope      = acos(|normal_z|)
  roughness  = sqrt(l0), thickness of the points around the plane

If the middle eigenvalue l1 is also tiny (sqrt(l1) < 1.5 cm) the points lie on a
line and the plane is not defined. This is what happens on the floor at long
range: with the camera about 0.43 m above the ground, two consecutive image rows
hit the floor 0.018*d^2 m apart (7 cm at 2 m, 16 cm at 3 m), so a 0.1 m cell far
away is crossed by a single row. Such a cell is skipped for this frame rather
than labelled from a meaningless plane.

A cell is labelled traversable when

  slope < 20 deg  and  roughness < 2 cm  and  no step

The step test compares the cell with its 8 neighbours observed in the same
frame: if the centroid of a neighbour is more than 5 cm away from the plane of
the cell, there is a step between them. It catches the edges of flat elevated
surfaces (seat of a sofa) that the slope alone would accept.

PCA was preferred over the z = ax + by + c regression of the previous homeworks
because most obstacle cells contain vertical surfaces (pillar sides, box faces,
walls), where the regression is degenerate. PCA handles any orientation and a
vertical surface simply gives 90 deg.

The labels live in a cv::Mat_<uint8_t> that already holds the OccupancyGrid
values (0 traversable, 100 not traversable, 0xFF read as -1 unknown), so
mat_to_og is a plain copy, as in wifi_map_base.


3. Bayesian model (objective 3)

State of a cell:          x in {T, N}   (traversable, not traversable)
Measurement in one frame: z in {t, n}   (output of the test of section 2)

Assumptions: the world is static, and the measurements are conditionally
independent given x.

Sensor model, symmetric, with a confidence that decreases with the distance r
(mean range of the points of the cell):

  P(z=t | T) = P(z=n | N) = p(r)
  P(z=t | N) = P(z=n | T) = 1 - p(r)

  p(r) = p_near + (p_far - p_near) * min(r / r_max, 1)
  p_near = 0.9,  p_far = 0.6,  r_max = 3 m

Bayes rule after the k-th measurement:

  P(x | z_1..k) = eta * P(z_k | x) * P(x | z_1..k-1)

The ratio of the two hypotheses eliminates eta:

  P(T | z_1..k)     P(z_k | T)     P(T | z_1..k-1)
  -------------  =  ----------  *  ---------------
  P(N | z_1..k)     P(z_k | N)     P(N | z_1..k-1)

and with l = log(P(T)/P(N)) the product becomes a sum:

  l_k = l_k-1 + log( P(z_k | T) / P(z_k | N) )
  l_0 = log(0.5 / 0.5) = 0            (uniform prior)

  z = t :  l_k = l_k-1 + log(p / (1-p))
  z = n :  l_k = l_k-1 - log(p / (1-p))

  P(T) = 1 - 1 / (1 + exp(l))

Weight of one measurement:

  r = 0      p = 0.90   +-2.20
  r = 1.5 m  p = 0.75   +-1.10
  r = 3 m    p = 0.60   +-0.41

so a measurement at 3 m counts about 5 times less than one at close range, which
is how the imprecision of long range updates is modelled.

Confidence: after k agreeing measurements with the same p,

  l = k log(p/(1-p)),   P(T) = p^k / (p^k + (1-p)^k)

it grows with the number of observations, as required.

Relation with the +1/-1 counter: the counter is exactly this filter with a
constant p = e/(1+e) = 0.731, the counter value being l. The Bayesian version
gives the counter a meaning (a probability) and lets the step depend on how
reliable the measurement is.

Clamping: l is kept in [-4.6, 4.6], i.e. P in [0.01, 0.99]. The cloud arrived
at 55 Hz in these runs and consecutive frames see a cell from almost the same
viewpoint, so the independence assumption is clearly wrong: without a bound
the filter becomes overconfident and a cell can never change again. With the
bound, a saturated cell changes label (crosses P = 0.3) after 3 contrary
measurements at close range, 5 at 1.5 m, or 14 at 3 m.

Outputs: ~/probability publishes 100 * P(N), so that the RViz "map" colour
scheme shows traversable cells white and obstacles black, with grey levels for
uncertain cells. ~/traversability gives the three labels: traversable if
P(T) > 0.7, not traversable if P(T) < 0.3, unknown otherwise or never observed.


4. Exploration

The road graph alone leaves holes, because the camera only looks forward along
the edges. A Scan task was added to floor_nav (tasks/TaskScan.cpp): the robot
rotates on the spot and accumulates |delta heading| until a full turn is done.
missions/explore_scan.py follows the Hamiltonian path of test_graph.py and does
a scan at the start and at every node.


5. Results

Simulation run headless, mission explore_scan.py, about 2.5 min. The ground
truth was computed from the heightfield and the object positions read from the
scene file. A band of one cell around every boundary (objects, cliffs, walls)
is excluded, since the label of a cell straddling a boundary depends on how the
points split inside it.

  observed cells               10325 of 12100 (the whole arena)
  correct, off boundaries      99.7 % of 8743 cells
  wrongly traversable          0
  wrongly not traversable      25, all just outside object footprints

The labels of objective 1 and the filtered map of objective 3 get the same
score here: the simulated depth has no noise, so the two only disagree on cells
whose label flips between frames. Most cells reach the bound of the filter
(P = 0.01 or 0.99) within a second of being seen at 55 Hz, which is why the
probability map looks almost binary at the end of the run. The few grey cells
left are those that really receive contradictory measurements.

Known limitation: the interior of a flat elevated surface is locally
indistinguishable from the floor; only its border is rejected by the step test.
The objects of this scene have vertical sides, so it has no effect here.


6. Parameters

  resolution      0.1 m       small enough for 0.2 m obstacles
  min/max_range   0.3 / 3 m   beyond 3 m most floor cells are skipped anyway
  min_points      6
  min_spread      0.015 m     needs at least two image rows in the cell
  max_slope_deg   20          ramp 13 deg, cliffs 30-60 deg
  max_roughness   0.02 m
  max_step        0.05 m
  p_near / p_far  0.9 / 0.6
  p_max           0.99        bound of the log-odds
  threshold       0.7         labels of ~/traversability
